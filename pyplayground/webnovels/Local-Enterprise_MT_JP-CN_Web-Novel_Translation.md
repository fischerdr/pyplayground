# Local & Enterprise MT for JP/CN Web-Novel Translation: A 2026 Survey and Strategy

## TL;DR
- **The strongest *new* self-hostable candidates for your Strix Halo boxes are Xiaomi's GemmaX2-28-9B, ByteDance's Seed-X-7B, and the SakuraLLM Qwen2.5 line — but for a JSON/glossary-reliable pipeline your best "native terminology intervention" option remains Tencent HY-MT1.5-7B, now joined conceptually by Alibaba's Qwen-MT (which has the feature but is API-only, so *not* self-hostable).** Seed-X is competitive on idiom/terminology but ByteDance itself warns its quantized/GGUF builds are unstable.
- **Enterprise and web-novel platforms have converged on the same pattern you're already using: an LLM (or specialized MT LLM) doing a first draft, plus a *deterministic glossary/term-injection layer* enforced outside the model** (Amazon/Microsoft/DeepL "custom terminology," Qwen/Hunyuan "term intervention," LNMTL's post-translation replacer). WTR-Lab runs on Google Gemini Flash with GPT-OSS-120B/Kimi fallbacks; J-Novel Club uses no AI at all.
- **On memory-bandwidth-limited unified-memory APUs (~215 GB/s measured), dense 12–14B translation models are your sweet spot at single-digit-to-teens tok/s; MoE models (GPT-OSS-120B, Qwen3-30B-A3B) run far faster and now fit your 96 GB box — making a two-model "draft small MoE + refine dense" ensemble realistic.** Your sentinel-masking approach is the correct architecture; adopt it as a model-independent layer.

## Key Findings

### 1. New self-hostable model candidates (beyond your existing list)
- **GemmaX2-28-9B (Xiaomi)** — Gemma-2-9B continual-pretrained MT model. Per Xiaomi's NAACL 2025 paper (Cui et al., arXiv:2502.02481): "GemmaX2-28 consistently outperforms the state-of-the-art (SOTA) models such as TowerInstruct and X-ALMA and achieves competitive performance with Google Translate and GPT-4-turbo" across 28 languages, and the authors call it "the open model with the highest translation quality." Supports JP + ZH + EN; GGUF quants exist; Gemma license. No native glossary feature — needs your masking approach. This is arguably the single best drop-in upgrade to test against TranslateGemma.
- **Seed-X-7B (ByteDance)** — Mistral-architecture 7B (~7.51B params), **OpenMDW** license (not Apache/MIT), purpose-built MT with STEM/code data deliberately excluded to specialize on translation. Family: Seed-X-Instruct-7B (CoT), Seed-X-PPO-7B (RL, the recommended translation variant), Seed-X-RM-7B (reward). Vendor claims parity with Gemini-2.5/Claude-3.5/GPT-4. The independent **DITING** web-novel benchmark (Wuhan Univ., arXiv:2510.09116) found Seed-X-PPO-7B "competitive on most linguistic dimensions but falter[s] on zero-pronoun recovery." **Critical caveat:** ByteDance posts an official warning that "quantized models are unstable" and recommends BF16 inference; GGUF users report garbled output, and the model officially wants beam-search decoding, which llama.cpp does not natively support (greedy decoding + low quants = gibberish risk). Run Q8 minimum if you try it.
- **SakuraLLM (Sakura-14B-Qwen2.5-v1.0, Sakura-GalTransl-7B-v3.7)** — the de facto community standard for JP→ZH light-novel/galgame translation. Qwen2.5-based, GGUF-native (14B iq4xs ≈ 8.2 GB), GalTransl-7B runs on 6 GB VRAM. Natively supports a "GPT dictionary" glossary "以保持专有名词和人称的一致性" (to maintain consistency of proper nouns and personal pronouns) — i.e., native terminology support. License CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial). Its target is JP→**Simplified Chinese**, not JP→English, so it fits your Chinese-reading workflows better than your English ones.
- **Aya Expanse 32B (Cohere)** — strong 23-language model (incl. JP, ZH, 128K context); a 2025 academic speech-translation study selected it over Gemma 3 27B for "stronger performance across more language pairs." Research/CC-BY-NC license (non-commercial). No native glossary. Fits your 48 GB box at 4-bit and your 96 GB box comfortably.
- **Others worth tracking:** Tower+ 9B (Unbabel, a top "translation-specific" pick in recent academic model selections), LMT (Qwen3-based Zh-centric multilingual MT, arXiv:2511.07003, 2026), and Sugoi 14B/32B (Mingshiba — JP→EN, visual-novel/light-novel trained, released via the Sugoi Toolkit Patreon; benchmarked on a full 4,569-line visual novel and reported to "completely solve MTL awkward wording issues").

### 2. What large players actually use for MT at scale
- **DeepL** — since July 2024 uses its own in-house specialized translation LLM (explicitly *not* GPT/Llama; built entirely in-house on 7+ years of proprietary translation data), with a Jan 2025 "next-gen" API model. Per DeepL's Jan 28 2025 press release: "Blind tests conducted with leading linguists found a remarkable 1.7x improvement with the new LLM against DeepL's old model for combinations involving English to Japanese and Simplified Chinese; and a 1.4x improvement for the combination of English and German." DeepL also claims its output is preferred "1.3x more often than Google Translate, 1.7x more often than ChatGPT-4, and 2.3x more often than Microsoft." Supports custom glossaries.
- **Google Translate** — an NMT core now augmented by Gemini/PaLM-family LLMs, plus a dedicated "Translation LLM" reported to be ~3× faster than Gemini with more human-like fluency. Gemini Flash is what WTR-Lab actually calls in production.
- **Amazon Translate & Microsoft Translator** — both offer deterministic **custom terminology / dictionaries** as an exact-match "copy-and-replace" layer applied around the neural model. Per Memsource/Phrase product manager Jan Hofmeister: "With MT Glossaries, we expect our customers to reduce the error rate for company-specific terminology by 80%." Amazon's 2023 enhancement even inflects matched terms (singular↔plural) for fluency; Microsoft's "neural phrase dictionary" adjusts both the term and its surrounding context. **This is exactly your sentinel-masking problem, solved as an external deterministic layer — the industry does not rely on model compliance alone.**
- **Light-novel publishers** — J-Novel Club uses **no AI**: a 9–15 week human translate/edit cycle plus ~4 weeks QA, modeled on early-2000s fansub workflows. The industry buzzword is **MTPE (Machine Translation Post-Editing)** — AI first draft, human polish. Manga publisher Orange Inc. uses AI-draft-then-human but says it would "NEVER publish anything that is only an AI-generated translation." TO Books (Japanese publisher of *Ascendance of a Bookworm*) partnered with the AI tool Corona EX. Per ANN's Nov 7 2025 survey, "68% out of 550 respondents said they were 'totally unaccepting' of AI being used in manga or anime translation" (concerns: mistranslation 90%, lost cultural references 83%, translator job loss 79%).

### 3. How web-novel MTL communities/sites do it
- **WTR-Lab** — the engine is **Google Gemini Flash** (Gemini 3 Flash primary, Gemini 2.5 Flash secondary), with a documented fallback chain to Moonshot's **Kimi K2 Instruct 0905** and then **OpenAI GPT-OSS-120B** during token shortages. It serves >100,000 chapters/day against a 200GB+ translation store, and is rolling out multi-model swapping (e.g. gemini-3.0-flash vs deepseek-chat) in the reader. Legacy Google/Bing/Yandex options remain for secondary languages. Notably, GPT-OSS-120B in their fallback stack is a model **you can self-host on your 96 GB box.**
- **LNMTL** — pioneered the community glossary pattern you're independently reinventing: an on-site glossary plus a **post-translation replacer** that swaps Chinese/English term pairs *after* the MT engine runs. Their own dev notes describe fixing cases like "小手" mistranslated as "microcheiria" → "small hands" and "美" as "US" → "beautiful" via post-translation replacement. Userscripts let readers layer their own Google-Sheets glossaries. This is essentially your sentinel/splice idea, and the community learned the same hard lesson: pure text-replacement breaks when the model splits or reorders term characters — hence the value of your sentinel-token masking, which is more robust than naive replacement.
- **Sugoi Toolkit** — offline JP→EN CNN/transformer models trained on visual novels + modern Japanese literary works; new 14B/32B LLM models are benchmarked on full visual novels (4,569 dialogue lines) and shared via Patreon then open-sourced a version later.
- **SakuraLLM ecosystem** (轻小说机翻机器人 / GalTransl / LunaTranslator) — the Chinese-language community's local-first stack, with GPT-dictionary glossary support and llama.cpp one-click packages. LunaTranslator natively supports local Sakura and OpenAI-compatible endpoints — meaning your llama-server can plug straight into it.
- **Amateur MTL recommendations** — Gemini Flash (huge context, cheap, near-free) for bulk drafts; ChatGPT/Claude for quality passes; DeepL+glossary for European pairs; and open tools like bilingual_book_maker (ChatGPT/Gemini/DeepL, `--use_context` to carry a context paragraph) and KazKozDev/book-translator (a two-stage Ollama pipeline: initial translation → AI self-reflection/refinement, which already defaults to `translategemma:12b`).

## Details

**Your hardware reality.** Strix Halo (gfx1151, Radeon 8060S, 40 CUs RDNA 3.5) has ~256 GB/s theoretical / ~215 GB/s measured LPDDR5X-8000 bandwidth over a 256-bit bus. Token generation is memory-bandwidth-bound: a dense 70B reads ~40 GB/token and caps around ~5 t/s, while a 30B MoE activating ~3B params reads far less and hits ~86–100 t/s on the same box. Community benchmarking (llama.cpp discussions #20856/#23659) shows **Vulkan/RADV now generally beats ROCm for decode** on this chip (e.g. 47.7→60.9 t/s just switching backends; +MTP speculative decode pushed one setup to ~85 t/s), while ROCm/HIP still wins prefill. Your 96 GB box can hold GPT-OSS-120B (MXFP4, ~60–65 GB) at ~55 t/s — a capability tier no single 24–32 GB dGPU can touch. Practical implication: for interactive drafting, favor Vulkan; for long-context batch chapter prefill, test a tuned ROCm build.

**Terminology intervention: native feature vs. external trick.** Models with *native* term-pinning (trained to honor a supplied glossary):
- **Tencent HY-MT1.5-1.8B / 7B** — "terminology intervention, contextual translation, and formatted translation" (open-sourced Dec 30 2025; the 7B is an upgrade of the WMT25 champion). GGUF-friendly, fits your 48 GB box trivially.
- **Alibaba Qwen-MT / Qwen3-MT** — has a native `translation_options.terms` glossary field ("users can predefine key terminology pairs and inject them as parameters"), fine-tuned from Qwen3 with a lightweight MoE backbone. **But it is API-only via Alibaba Cloud Model Studio/DashScope — no weights, no GGUF, not self-hostable.** Useful only as a design reference or cloud fallback.
- **SakuraLLM** — GPT-dictionary glossary, native, GGUF, local. JP→ZH.

Everything else you listed or I found (GemmaX2, Seed-X, Aya, Gemma 3, Mistral Small, NLLB-200, TowerInstruct, Sugoi) has **no native glossary** and requires your external masking/splicing. The enterprise NMT services (Amazon, Microsoft, DeepL, Google) all implement glossaries as a **deterministic layer around the model**, not as model magic — which strongly validates your sentinel-token architecture as the right design regardless of which model you run.

**Quality expectations for raw MTL.** ANN quotes *Ascendance of a Bookworm* translator Quof: "high translation quality is basically never a factor in how well something sells… You only really need a decent translation." MTL sites thrive on "decent right now" beating "great in five years." For CN→EN web-novel prose specifically, DITING (arXiv:2510.09116, 14 models, 18K+ expert-annotated sentence pairs) concluded: "DeepSeek-V3 produces the most faithful and stylistically coherent translations, Chinese-trained LLMs consistently outperform larger foreign counterparts, and state-of-the-art (SOTA) LLMs surpass commercial MT systems." The consistent weak point across small specialized models is **discourse-level coherence (zero-pronoun recovery)** — exactly where carrying cross-chapter context helps most.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Immediate (this week):** Keep TranslateGemma-12B as baseline. Add **HY-MT1.5-7B** as your terminology-critical engine — it has native intervention, is GGUF, and fits the 48 GB box with room to spare. Build a fixed, dialogue-heavy JP→EN and CN→EN evaluation chapter set (with your glossary) and score both models on (a) literary fluency, (b) name/term consistency, (c) sentinel/JSON survival rate. That harness is what all later decisions hinge on.

**Stage 2 — Evaluate the new models:** Pull GGUF quants of **GemmaX2-28-9B** (highest-priority; strongest independent MT pedigree) and **Seed-X-PPO-7B** (Q8 minimum, watch for gibberish; if unstable, drop it). Run them through the Stage-1 harness. Keep whichever most reliably survives your sentinel-masking, not merely whichever reads best.

**Stage 3 — Exploit the 96 GB box with an ensemble:** Run a **two-stage pipeline** mirroring Hunyuan-Chimera's ensemble-refinement and the book-translator two-stage pattern: a fast MoE (GPT-OSS-120B or Qwen3-30B-A3B) produces the raw draft at ~55–100 t/s, then a dense specialized MT model (HY-MT1.5 or GemmaX2/TranslateGemma) does a refinement + glossary-consistency pass. This uses your bandwidth-rich-but-latency-limited hardware exactly where each model type is strongest.

**Stage 4 — Borrow enterprise techniques regardless of model:**
- Formalize your glossary as a **deterministic post-translation replacer** (LNMTL/Amazon model) layered *on top of* in-prompt term injection, so consistency never depends solely on model compliance. Your sentinel-token masking is more robust than LNMTL's naive replace — keep it, but add a final deterministic verification/replace pass.
- Add **quality-estimation gating** (the automatic-post-editing patent pattern): score each segment (COMET-style or an LLM-judge), and only re-translate segments below a threshold — cheaper than re-running whole chapters.
- Maintain a **running character/terminology bible carried across chapters** to fight the documented context-loss failure (character described differently in ch. 15 than ch. 2). This directly targets the zero-pronoun/discourse weakness DITING flagged in small models.

**Benchmarks/thresholds that would change the plan:** If Seed-X GGUF garbling can't be tamed at Q8 with tuned sampling, abandon it. If the MoE-draft + dense-refine pass doesn't beat single-model dense on your rubric by a meaningful margin, stay single-model to halve latency. If HY-MT1.5's native intervention beats your masking on name-consistency scores, migrate the glossary enforcement into it. If a future open-weight Qwen-MT or a DeepSeek-V3 distillation lands with a permissive license, re-test — DITING suggests Chinese-trained models lead on CN→EN.

## Caveats
- Several 2026 "best model" listicle sources (SiliconFlow, noviai, hakunamatatatech, promptquorum) are SEO/vendor content citing speculative future model versions (GPT-5.x, Gemini 3.5, Qwen3.6); treat their *rankings* as marketing, not evidence. The concrete, checkable facts (release dates, licenses, benchmark quotes) come from primary sources (arXiv, HuggingFace model cards, vendor press releases, ANN reporting).
- **Qwen-MT's native terminology feature is real but API-only** — you cannot self-host it; it is a design reference, not a deployment option.
- **Seed-X, Aya Expanse, and SakuraLLM carry non-commercial/research licenses** — fine for a personal homelab, not for any paid/public service.
- **DITING is CN→EN only.** No equivalent independent JP→EN *literary* benchmark was found; vendor JP claims (DeepL, Seed-X, GemmaX2) are largely news/general-domain (FLORES-200, WMT), so JP→EN literary quality still requires your own eval harness.
- WTR-Lab's fallback-chain details come partly from Grokipedia (lower-reliability); the core Gemini Flash usage is corroborated by WTR-Lab's own site copy and changelog.
- ByteDance's own "quantized models are unstable" warning on Seed-X is the single most important operational risk in this report for a llama.cpp/GGUF shop — validate output integrity before trusting it in a pipeline.
